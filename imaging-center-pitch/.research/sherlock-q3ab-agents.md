# Q3a/Q3b — Agentic Automation of Radiology/Healthcare ADMINISTRATIVE Workflow

**Research date:** 2026-10-10
**Scope:** Agentic automation of healthcare ADMINISTRATIVE workflow (auth, RCM, scheduling, patient comms, documentation). Clinical/diagnostic image AI **excluded** by instruction.
**Method:** ~30 WebSearch/WebFetch calls. Every claim below carries source + date + confidence. Vendor marketing is labeled **VENDOR CLAIM**. Measured results are labeled **MEASURED**; forward-looking numbers **PROJECTED**.

> **Standing caution for this project:** a large share of the "measured outcome" literature in this space is vendor-published case studies. I have labeled these as VENDOR CLAIM even when they contain specific percentages, because the measurement, baseline, and selection of the reported cohort are all controlled by the seller. Genuinely independent (peer-reviewed or regulator-published) outcome data for *administrative* agentic AI is **close to nonexistent as of Oct 2026** — see "What I Could NOT Verify."

---

## 0. Executive answer to the buyer question

For a **single-site outpatient imaging center**, the vendor landscape splits cleanly into three tiers, and the tier matters more than the feature list:

| Tier | Who | Single-site can buy? |
|---|---|---|
| **Payer-funded / payer-contracted** | Cohere Health, Anterior, Availity (base tier), Rhyme (increasingly) | **No — cannot buy. Arrives via the payer.** Free to provider, but serves payer's interest. |
| **Enterprise provider-side** | Notable, Hyro, Abridge, Ambience, SmarterDx, Latent, Waystar, R1/Humata, Parlance, Syllable, Infinitus | **No / impractical.** Health-system sales motion, 5–6 figure floors, no self-serve. |
| **Plausibly reachable** | Candid Health (% of collections), Honey Health (per-PA), Alpha Nodus via RamSoft RIS/PACS, Staffingly (service not software), Luma Health, Artera, Tennr, Assort (at the $75K floor) | **Yes, with caveats.** See §1 detail. |

The single most strategically relevant finding for an imaging center is **not** a vendor at all — it is that **payers are deploying AI against routine radiology claims post-service, pre-payment**, coding denials as *provider liability* (radiologybusiness.com, Sept 2026 — see §3). That reframes the pitch: the center needs agentic capability defensively, to contest an automated counterparty.

## 4. Q3b — Human-in-the-loop: evidence, failures, and deployed architectures

### 4.1 Documented failure modes of full automation

#### UnitedHealth / NaviHealth — nH Predict (*Estate of Lokken v. UnitedHealth Group*, D. Minn., No. 0:23-cv-03514)
- **Allegation:** UHG and naviHealth used **nH Predict** to deny post-acute care coverage based on **population-level algorithms rather than individual clinical circumstances**, overriding treating physicians' recommendations. Plaintiffs allege **~90% of these denials were overturned on appeal.** — **Confidence: High** (allegation); **Medium** (the 90% figure is a pleading, not an adjudicated finding)
- **Procedural history:**
  - **Feb 13 2025** — court **denied** UHC's motion to dismiss as to certain state-law claims; **breach of contract and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing proceed.** ([Tressler LLP](https://www.tresslerllp.com/thought-leadership/estate-of-gene-b-lokken-et-al-v-unitedhealth-group-inc-ai-risks-in-medical-insurance-coverage-disputes/); [legalhie](https://www.legalhie.com/judge-decides-class-action-lawsuit-can-proceed-against-unitedhealth-for-use-of-ai/)) — **Confidence: High**
  - **Mar 9 2026** — federal **magistrate judge ordered broad discovery**, compelling production of internal documents on nH Predict's **implementation, use, and outcomes**. ([Order on Motion to Compel Discovery, Georgetown Litigation Tracker PDF](https://litigationtracker.law.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Estate-of-Gene-B.-Lokken-et-al-v.-UnitedHealth-Group-Inc.-et-al_2026_3_9_ORDER-ON-MOTION-TO-COMPEL-DISCOVERY.pdf); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/hunton-insurance-recovery-blog/court-allows-discovery-into-insurers-use-of-ai-to-deny-claims)) — **Confidence: High**
  - **Status Oct 2026:** in discovery; **no trial date set; no liability finding.** — **Confidence: High**
- **The transferable lesson:** the legal exposure attached to **using a population-level model in place of individualized review, and to the absence of meaningful human examination of the record** — not to AI per se.

#### Cigna — PxDx ("procedure-to-diagnosis")
- **Reported conduct (ProPublica, March 2023):** Cigna allowed **medical directors to deny large batches of claims without reviewing individual member files.** Over **two months in 2022, PxDx auto-reviewed and denied over 300,000 claims — averaging ~1.2 seconds per claim.** — **Confidence: High** (widely corroborated across trade press)
- **Litigation:**
  - **July 2023** — California class action (two Cigna members); theory: many states, incl. California, **require a physician to review the patient file and coverage policy before a medical denial**, which PxDx bypassed. ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/cigna-lawsuit-algorithm-claims-denials-california/688857/)) — **Confidence: High**
  - **Late Aug 2023** — second putative nationwide class action, D. Conn. ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/cigna-lawsuit-claims-algorithm-denials-connecticut/694292/)) — **Confidence: High**
  - **Mar 31 2025** — **E.D. Cal. allowed the PxDx class action to proceed.** — **Confidence: Medium-High**
- **🔴 Directly imaging-relevant:** **a lawsuit alleges Cigna violated the law by denying claims for imaging and other services in bulk.** ([Radiology Business](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/healthcare-management/healthcare-economics/cigna-violated-law-denying-claims-imaging-and-other-services-bulk-lawsuit-alleges)) — **Confidence: Medium.** Imaging was *named* in the bulk-denial allegations.
- **The transferable lesson:** **1.2 seconds per claim is the number that makes "human review" indefensible.** The failure mode is **nominal** human review — a signature with no examination. Any HIL design must therefore produce evidence of *actual* review, not just a reviewer field.

#### CMS WISeR Model — the federal government's own AI prior-auth pilot
- **What:** **Wasteful and Inappropriate Service Reduction (WISeR) Model** — AI-assisted prior authorization / pre-payment review in **Original Medicare**. Announced June 2025; **active Jan 1 2026**; **six-year** pilot. — **Confidence: High**
- **States: Arizona, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas, Washington.** ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/wiser-under-scrutiny-ai-claims-review-debate-reaches-cms); [Dinsmore](https://www.dinsmore.com/publications/is-ai-wiser-cms-models-ai-based-prior-authorization-process-in-six-states/)) — **Confidence: High**. **Note: Texas is included** — relevant if the imaging center's book touches Original Medicare in TX.
- **Services:** high-risk/overused items — **skin and tissue substitutes, electrical nerve stimulator implants**; reported as **13 service categories** (with 17 outpatient services identified as overused/high-risk). **Imaging is NOT identified as in scope** in the sources I reached. — **Confidence: Medium** (that imaging is out of scope for now); I could **not** obtain the authoritative CMS service list.
- **🟢 The human-review mandate — the single best piece of Q3b evidence:** **CMS requires a human clinician to review every decision not to affirm coverage**; six private companies use AI to assist, but **"all recommendations for non-payment are determined by appropriately licensed clinicians."** ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/wiser-under-scrutiny-ai-claims-review-debate-reaches-cms), 2026) — **Confidence: High.** This is **CMS itself establishing that AI may recommend but a licensed human must decide an adverse determination** — the strongest available authority for the HIL architecture.
- **Contested outcome data:** EFF alleges the model produces inappropriately high denial rates — **only 62% of AI denials approved on human review vs. 84% in traditional Medicare Advantage**. ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/wiser-under-scrutiny-ai-claims-review-debate-reaches-cms)) — **Confidence: Low-Medium** (advocacy-group figure, contested, methodology unseen). **Do not present as established.**
- **Pushback:** **EFF filed a March 2026 lawsuit** seeking transparency on WISeR's design and safeguards; the debate has reached CMS. — **Confidence: Medium**

### 4.2 Actual deployed review architectures (published descriptions, not theory)

Five concrete patterns, each with a source:

1. **Confidence threshold + dual queue (autonomous coding).** Confidence values route each chart to a **"Confident" queue** (auto-submitted) or a **"Review" queue** (human). **The threshold is set manually with the customer, typically >95% — explicitly higher than typical inter-coder agreement between experienced human coders.** ([asprcmsolutions](https://asprcmsolutions.com/blog/autonomous-medical-coding/)) — **Confidence: Medium.** *This is the most directly reusable architecture in the report.*
2. **Role inversion: coders become auditors and exception-handlers.** With autonomous coding, the coder's role **evolves into auditor and exception coder** — reviewing AI work through a **built-in QA process** and concentrating expertise on complex cases that **don't qualify for automation**. ([mdaudit](https://mdaudit.com/blog/auditing-autonomous-medical-coding-systems/)) — **Confidence: Medium.** Staffing implication: HIL is a *redeployment*, not a headcount add.
3. **Pre-bill human validation as the product design (SmarterDx).** AI "surfaces defensible revenue and quality findings **your team validates**" — the human validation step is the shipped workflow, not an option. ([smarterdx.com](https://www.smarterdx.com/)) — **Confidence: High**
4. **Pre-deployment simulation / "LLM as Judge" (Artera).** A **separate LLM runs simulations before any agent goes live**, verifying the agent achieves the intended outcome (reduced call volume, increased care-plan compliance) before it touches patients. ([artera.io](https://artera.io/blog/ai-agents-for-patient-communications-himss-recap/), HIMSS26) — **Confidence: Medium.** Pattern: **validate the agent, not just the output.**
5. **Deterministic escalation + context handoff.** Hyro **routes anything clinical or urgent to a human** by design ([getmacha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/hyro-complete-guide)); Parlance ships an **AI-generated call summary that briefs the live agent at the moment of transfer** ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/parlance-gives-healthcare-contact-centers-instant-configuration-control-and-ai-briefed-agent-transfers-302839592.html), 2026). — **Confidence: Medium.** Pattern: the handoff must carry context, or the human restarts from zero and the automation is net-negative.
6. **Vendor-side clinician review team (Anterior).** Anterior pairs its AI with a **clinician-led team** supporting payers on accuracy — the HIL is staffed by the vendor. — **Confidence: Medium**

### 4.4 Synthesis — what the evidence actually supports

1. **The legal exposure attaches to three specific things**, repeatedly, across Lokken, PxDx, and all eight statutes: **(a)** using population-level inference in place of the individual record; **(b)** adverse determinations with no genuine human examination; **(c)** nominal review (the 1.2-second signature). It does **not** attach to AI assistance as such.
2. **The emerging consensus standard is narrow and consistent: AI may draft, score, route and recommend; a licensed human must decide anything adverse, and must demonstrably examine the record.** CMS's own WISeR design, California SB 1120, and AMA D-480.956 all land in the same place.
3. **Most published obligations bind payers, not providers — with three exceptions a provider must heed:** Texas TRAIGA (patient disclosure, incl. scheduling and chatbots), Indiana HB 1271 (provider must review AI-prepared claims pre-submission), and Texas SB 1188 (review of AI-created records). Plus the general upcoding/False-Claims exposure that OIG's Feb 2026 MA ICPG now explicitly ties to AI-generated coding prompts.
4. **The deployed architecture that has actual published description is: confidence threshold (>95%) → auto-submit vs. exception queue → coders redeployed as auditors → sampling QA → documented pre-deployment validation.** That is the defensible pattern, and it is defensible precisely because it generates evidence of review.
5. **For an imaging center, the asymmetry is the strategic point:** the payer side is deploying AI against your claims (§3) under statutes that mostly regulate *them*, while your own obligations are disclosure and pre-submission review. Agentic automation on the provider side is best framed as **restoring parity against an automated counterparty**, with HIL as the thing that keeps it defensible rather than the thing that slows it down.
