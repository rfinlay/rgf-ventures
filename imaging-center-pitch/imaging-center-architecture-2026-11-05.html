Constraint, the most fragile item

No portal screen-driving against PHI.

The AI vendor's BAA excludes Computer Use and Web Fetch, the two capabilities one would use to drive a payer portal with no API. So the third path is a human, by design. A locally hosted model is a specification-phase option for that gap. It is not promised.
Graduation criterion, written in the specification

Every item, until the owner says otherwise.

Sign-off moves from every item to a sample only after a defined volume at a defined accuracy, measured, and only with the owner approving the change. Until then, every submission and every appeal carries a human signature.
The FDA line, inside this pipeline

Claims, billing, eligibility. Never appropriateness.

"Claims or billing information" and "determination of health benefit eligibility" are named in the administrative support exclusion, 21 U.S.C. 360j(o)(1)(A). The agent never recommends which study to order or whether a study is clinically appropriate. That would leave the exclusion.

The FDA line

Nothing interprets an image, selects a protocol, or recommends a study.

Administrative support software is excluded from the device definition at 21 U.S.C. 360j(o)(1)(A): appointment schedules, claims or billing information, business analytics, practice and inventory management, determination of health benefit eligibility. Every service here lives inside that list. The moment software touches image data or clinical appropriateness it leaves the exclusion and enters Clinical Decision Support territory at 360j(o)(1)(E). Candidate #13 is declined for that reason, out loud.
The BAA envelope

What is inside, what is not.

A business associate agreement with the center before any PHI flows, 45 CFR 164.502(e). The AI vendor's agreement covers Skills, Web Search, Memory, Bash tool, and Text Editor tool. It excludes Computer Use and Web Fetch, so payer-portal screen-driving against PHI is not available. Confirm the applicable BAA version in writing.
Texas SB 1188

US data residency, every component.

Electronic health records with Texas patient data must be physically stored in the US as of 2026-01-01. This binds the RIS, the PACS, the billing vendor, the voice vendor's telephony and transcripts, and the model provider. Verify each one at selection. Texas HB 300 likely reaches Rick directly as a covered entity; its exact intervals are not quoted here because they were not verified.

The default: one environment, one contract, isolated instances

Security first, and the cost stays low.

The isolation lives at the data layer, where a regulator, an auditor, or a partner's lawyer expects to find it: separate databases, separate encryption keys, separate logins, separate audit logs. The environment is operated, patched and monitored once. One owner or one joint venture owning every center: this is the whole answer. A report that spans organizations is a later, explicit decision by all the principals, built read-only, never as a merged record.
The variant: separately owned, fully separate hosting

A fully separate stack, at higher cost, as that owner's choice.

A separately owned center whose owner wants its own hosting and its own books gets a stack of its own: its own host, environment, contract and bills. It adds nothing for security that the isolated instance does not already give; it adds separation of books and billing, which is a legitimate reason and not a security argument. Either works. What is never built is one shared database with a center column.

The demo, as built. Left to right, one order.
1. RIS order
SIMULATED
A synthetic HL7 v2.5.1 ORM^O01 message, generated per order and displayed. No live RIS connected.
2. Prior auth agent
REAL
Checks the auth rule, assembles a Da Vinci PAS FHIR bundle (Claim, ServiceRequest, QuestionnaireResponse, Patient, Coverage, more). A person approves it first.
3. HL7 Da Vinci PAS reference sandbox
REAL
POST Claim/$submit to prior-auth.davinci.hl7.org returns HTTP 201 and a tracking number; a GET reads the stored ClaimResponse back.
4. Payer decision
SIMULATED
The sandbox has no imaging rules, so approve or deny is scripted and labeled.
5. Appeal
DRAFTED, HUMAN SIGNS
Drafted from the denial and notes; the physician edits and signs.
6. Monday report
SYNTHETIC
Turnaround, denial, appeal, overturn rates; placeholder numbers.
HUMAN SIGN-OFF: packet approval before step 3  ·  appeal signature at step 5. The agent never submits or signs on its own. CMS clocks (7 days standard, 72 hours expedited) are real logic on a fast-forward demo clock.
The production path. What Phase 4 (build and configure) swaps in.
Your RIS
The real RIS through its vendor's HL7 and API sandbox (Phase 4), then production. Orders arrive as HL7 v2 ORM; results back as ORU. Contract terms one and two.
Prior auth agent, in production
Same pipeline. The model vendor operates under a signed BAA, US data residency. Drafts, checks, and tracks; never screen-drives a payer portal. One instance per center, its own audit log.
Real payers
FHIR PAS where the payer runs it (Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, exchange plans from 2027-01-01). EDI 278 through the clearinghouse where not. A human-assisted path for fax and portal payers. Test access first (Phase 4).
Payer decision
Real ClaimResponse from the payer, on the CMS clock. The agent schedules on approval.
Appeal
Model draft under the BAA. The radiologist or ordering physician signs. Nothing outbound unsigned.
Monday report, two views
Operations and costs for the owners, revenue for the practice, calculated and checked. The owner's agent answers by text or call.
Human sign-off points, production

A person stays in charge at every gate that leaves the building.

  • Your coordinator approves each prior authorization packet before submission.
  • A physician signs every appeal; the clinical argument is theirs.
  • The radiologist signs every report (RadAssist, unchanged: calcium, thyroid, and spine all draft from his template and his rules; nothing interprets an image).
  • The owner approves the sign-off rules for each center, and any change to them.
  • Graduation from approve-every-item to sampled review happens only when the owner says so, in writing.
What makes production possible

The dependencies on page 7, mapped to this diagram.

  • RIS box: RIS and PACS selected; HL7, API, and DICOM sandbox access granted; the four contract terms signed.
  • Agent box: HIPAA BAAs executed with the model vendor and every vendor touching patient data; US residency verified.
  • Payer box: payer FHIR and EDI test access; payer contracts and credentialing; payer mix known before payer-specific rules.
  • Report box: real volumes after opening; until then, the report runs on test data.
  • Vendor-neutral throughout: works with whatever RIS and PACS the center picks.
